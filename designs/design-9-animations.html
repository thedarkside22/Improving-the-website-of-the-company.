Engineering the invisible

Air that knows which way to go.

The difference between a cleanroom and a containment lab is direction. Pressure cascades push clean air out to protect the product — or pull air in to protect everyone outside. Same physics, opposite intent.

+ Positive pressure · Cleanroom

Higher pressure inside means air flows outward through every gap — nothing drifts in to contaminate the product.

− Negative pressure · Containment (BSL-3/4)

Lower pressure inside means air is drawn inward and exhausted through HEPA — hazardous agents can't escape to the outside.

Equip · Biological safety cabinets

The invisible barrier, made visible.

A Class II biological safety cabinet protects three things at once — the sample, the operator, and the room — using nothing but precisely directed air. Hover a stream to trace what it protects.

Downflow → product protection

Clean HEPA air falls over the work surface so nothing lands on the sample.

Inflow → operator protection

Air is drawn in at the opening, so aerosols never reach the person.

HEPA exhaust → environment protection

Everything leaves through a HEPA filter, protecting the room and the world outside.

Honest by design: this is a Class II cabinet. A laminar-flow clean bench protects the product only, and a fume hood controls chemical vapour — neither is a substitute. We label every product for exactly what it protects.
